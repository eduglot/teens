Stage 2: Presentation & Dialogue 15 Mins

Part A: Dialogue Role-Play (Toby & Chloe)

Toby: Hey Chloe, why are you sitting over here looking so upset?
Chloe: I was honestly seeing red five minutes ago! Mark has been getting on my nerves all morning by tapping his ruler on my desk.
Toby: Oh no, that tapping sound would drive someone crazy! Did you ask him to stop?
Chloe: I did, but he got an attitude with me and rolled his eyes! Then our teacher had to get on to him for causing a distraction during silent reading.
Toby: Mark can be really hot-tempered sometimes. Did he apologize to you afterwards[cite: 2]?
Chloe: At first he just mumbled "Whatever," which showed zero remorse. But after the teacher spoke to him, he came over and offered a real apology.
Toby: What did he say? Was it sincere?
Chloe: Yes, he said: "I apologize for being rude earlier. It's my fault, and I didn't mean to bother you. Please accept my apologies."
Toby: Wow, he took full responsibility! It takes courage to say "my bad" or "my mistake" and truly mean it. Are you guys okay now?
Chloe: Yep! Once someone gives a sincere apology, I never hold a grudge.

Part B: Click Cards to Study Expressions

Seeing red

Feeling extremely furious and angry!

Drive crazy

To annoy or irritate someone intensely.

Get on nerves

To frustrate or bother someone over and over.

Hot-tempered

Getting angry very quickly or easily.

Get on to someone

To scold or reprimand someone for bad actions.

Get an attitude

Acting disrespectful, sulky, or rude.

Remorse

Feeling genuine regret for hurting someone.

Sincere

Honest, genuine, and from the heart.

Take responsibility

Admitting you caused the problem.

My bad / My fault

Casual and honest ways to admit a quick slip-up.

Hold a grudge

Staying angry at someone for a long time.

Please accept my apologies

A polite and formal apology phrase.
